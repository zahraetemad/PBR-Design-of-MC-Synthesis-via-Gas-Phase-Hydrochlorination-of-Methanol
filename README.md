A packed-bed reactor model for methyl chloride production from methanol hydrochlorination was developed for the Design Project, which was part of the MEng in Chemical Engineering degree at the University of Manchester.
A first-principles reactor model was developed by coupling mass, energy, and momentum balances. Temperature-dependent thermodynamic properties were calculated using Shomate equations to evaluate reaction enthalpy, entropy, and equilibrium constants. Reaction kinetics were implemented using literature-based LHHW-type rate expressions for both the main and side reactions. The packed-bed pressure drop was modelled using the Ergun equation, and heat removal was incorporated via a coolant energy balance. The resulting system of nonlinear differential equations was solved numerically using scipy.solve_ivp to obtain reactor performance profiles, including conversion, temperature, pressure, and heat duty.
Reaction kinetics are intrinsic and were obtained from published work by Becerra et al. for the hydrochlorination of methanol over γ-alumina catalytic mechanism and Bercic and Levec for the catalytic dehydration of methanol to DME over γ-alumina.
